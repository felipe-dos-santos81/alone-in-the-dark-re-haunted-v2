# Music prompts

One block per piece of the external soundtrack, ready to paste into a music
generator. The brief behind them is
[music-generation-guide.md](music-generation-guide.md): what each number
scores, where the lengths come from, and how the files reach the game.

Each block gives the file name, the target length (within about 10 percent),
a **Style** paragraph for the generator's style or description field, and
the **Source text** it was written from, quoted from the game's English with
its page and layout codes stripped. The two vocal pieces, 02 and 03, add
**Lyrics**: a draft the generation project may rewrite, kept to the words of
the source. Everything else is instrumental. Period: 1924, Louisiana. Master
the whole set to one loudness level.

## 02 - Epilogue credits (`02.ogg`, 1:20, plays once)

**Style.** Orchestral farewell with a solo voice, slow, in a major key that
keeps a shadow. Dawn light. Strings and a piano, a sung verse, then the
voice gives way to the orchestra for the credits. Not a dirge: a man at
peace with what he did. Ends resolved.

**Lyrics** (draft).

```
They will find my body
But will not have my soul.
I hear their footsteps,
The sun has set.

Some may understand what I have done.
May God forgive me.
Farewell, farewell.
```

**Source text.** Jeremy Hartwood's note (`doc01.txt`):

> They are coming. I have freed hellish forces and now the price must be
> paid. Derceto is the prey of evil. The sun has set.
> They will find my body but will not have my soul.
> I can imagine the master's fury and the terror in the hearts of his slaves.
> I hear their footsteps.
> Some may understand what I have done.
> May God forgive me. Farewell.
> Jeremy Hartwood.

## 03 - Intro (`03.ogg`, 1:17, plays once)

**Style.** 1920s road at dusk into a haunted house: a slow drive, a walk up
the steps, a climb to the attic. Begins almost as a period song, a woman's
voice over a small ensemble (piano, strings, a clarinet), and darkens floor
by floor into unease. One file plays for both heroes, so the voice is
Emily's, or a narrator's that fits either. Cut short by the game when play
starts, so no tail needed.

**Lyrics** (draft).

```
The kind of place ghosts run away from,
Murders, curses, lunacy.
Derceto is waiting for me.

I pray my fear is nothing more
Than the fruit of my imagination.
Derceto is waiting for me.
```

**Source text.** Edward Carnby's case notes (`doc20.txt`):

> I've been reading up on the history of the old house; it's the kind of
> place ghosts run away from in terror. Grisly murders, curses, lunacy ...

Emily Hartwood's letter (`doc21.txt`):

> Now is the time to confront the mysteries. Derceto is waiting for me. I
> pray that my fear is nothing more than the fruit of my imagination.

## 04 - The attic theme, calm restored (`04.ogg`, 2:08, plays once)

**Style.** Uneasy calm. Slow, sparse, a music box or celesta over low
strings, a clock ticking somewhere in the harmony, long held notes. The most
played piece in the game: it opens the game and returns after every fight,
so it must bear repetition and share a key or a motif with 05. No climax.

**Source text.** Emily Hartwood's letter (`doc21.txt`):

> That creaking old mansion, with its unusual tales, its secret library
> door, the ancient upstairs clock, all those occult books

## 05 - Danger (`05.ogg`, 1:44, plays once)

**Style.** Driving fight music, mid tempo, low brass and percussion,
strings in short strokes, no melody that resolves. It follows a sting (12,
13 or 14) and gives way to 04 when the enemy dies, so it shares their key.
Keeps tension flat rather than building to an end.

**Source text.** "The Creatures of Night" (`doc09.txt`):

> You who read me, know that night engenders monsters and that night
> creatures exist.

## 07 - House haunting cue (`07.ogg`, 2:03, plays once)

**Style.** Sparse and cold. Long silences, a breath of strings, a single
piano note, a far creak. Plays rarely and unannounced in the house as a
presence passing, not a scene. Half of the piece may be near silence.

**Source text.** Jeremy Hartwood's diary, February 7 (`doc07.txt`):

> His terrible smile will haunt me to my dying day. His breath was ice and
> his burning eyes froze me; I could not move!

## 08 - Death (`08.ogg`, 0:34, plays once)

**Style.** One phrase, then nothing. A low choir or brass swell, a drum,
silence. The hero's body is carried to the tree in the cavern and the game
ends. No hope in it.

**Source text.** Pregzt (`doc18.txt`); "The End" is the screen's caption:

> My servants will lay you upon the sacrificial stone. My roar will rend the
> night. You will be mine and I shall reign once more. Come to me.

## 09 - Epilogue, the walk out (`09.ogg`, 1:29, plays once)

**Style.** Dawn after a night. Slow, warm strings and a solo woodwind,
relief with a shadow in it, the house behind. Leads into 02, so ends open
rather than final and shares its key.

**Source text.** Emily Hartwood's letter (`doc21.txt`):

> Nothing will ever persuade me that my uncle was insane.

## 10 - The ending, fire (`10.ogg`, 1:23, plays once)

**Style.** Climax. Full orchestra and choir, fire and collapse: the lamp
thrown, the tree burning, the cavern shaking. Starts at full force, breaks
up in the last 20 seconds into rumble and falling stone as the hero runs.

**Source text.** The Book of Abdul (`doc03.txt`); "Get out of here!" is the
line shouted as the cavern shakes:

> Let the shadow of Cthulhu darken the sky.

## 11 - Caves haunting cue (`11.ogg`, 3:20, plays once)

**Style.** Drums far off in a swamp at night, a sea shanty half heard,
drowned voices. Slow, dark, low percussion and a bowed drone; an optional
chanted version may use the eight lines below. Like 07 it plays rarely and
unannounced, underground.

**Source text.** The pirates' song and the trial's last night (`doc17.txt`):

> A skull! Go to port
> Saber! To starboard!
> Pass over that will
> And with death you'll deal.
> If you cut a rope,
> Cut the right I hope,
> Or then, I don't mind
> The death you will find.

> My snoring companions didn't hear the insidious rhythm of far-off drums.

## 12 - Fight sting A (`12.ogg`, 0:25, plays once)

**Style.** A hit, a rise, and a hand-over: an orchestral stab, a rising
string line, ending on the chord 05 begins on. The window zombie's
entrance, among others.

**Source text.** "The Creatures of Night" (`doc09.txt`):

> He who prowls among books will perish by the blade.

## 13 - Fight sting B (`13.ogg`, 0:11, plays once)

**Style.** Shorter and harder than 12: two stabs and a snarl of brass, then
straight into 05's key.

**Source text.** "Of Libraries" (`doc09.txt`):

> Unhappy he who frees the prowler.

## 14 - Fight sting C (`14.ogg`, 0:09, plays once)

**Style.** The shortest: a scream of strings and one drum hit, into 05's
key. The dance hall ghosts and the cave creatures.

**Source text.** "The Creatures of Night" (`doc09.txt`):

> He who flies in the dark caverns will scream in fear.

## 15 - Gramophone record, The Blue Danube (`15.ogg`, 2:51, plays once)

**Style.** Johann Strauss II, The Blue Danube (1866, public domain), as a
1920s shellac record played on a gramophone: narrow band, horn tone,
surface hiss, slight wow. The player hears it come out of the horn in the
room, so period sound matters more than fidelity. Instrumental, no score
around it.

**Source text.** The game's caption: "You're listening to the Blue Danube".

## 16 - Gramophone record, Chopin (`16.ogg`, 1:39, plays once)

**Style.** Frédéric Chopin, Waltz in A-flat major Op. 69 No. 1 (1835, public
domain), solo piano, on the same gramophone as 15.

**Source text.** The game's caption: "You're listening to the Posthumous
Opus 69 Nr 1".

## 17 - Gramophone record, Danse macabre (`17.ogg`, 3:33, plays once)

**Style.** Camille Saint-Saëns, Danse macabre (1874, public domain), on the
same gramophone as 15. The record the dance hall's ghosts answer to.

**Source text.** The game's captions: "You're listening to the Dance of
Death" and, from the ghosts, "They seem to dislike this music".

## 18 - Title theme (`18.ogg`, 1:30, seamless loop)

**Style.** The game's main theme, heard under the startup menu. Slow and
grand, strings and a low choir, the motif of 04 stated plainly, a 1920s
shadow in the harmony. Loops seamlessly: the end leads back into the start
with no silence or cadence.

**Source text.** None; the theme stands for the whole game. Jeremy's note
(02) is the mood to write from.

## 19 - Ambient loop, the house (`19.ogg`, 3:00, seamless loop)

**Style.** Near-silent ambience for the four floors of the house: a held
low string, wind in a chimney, a clock, a floorboard, no melody. Plays
whenever no scripted song does, so it must disappear into the room. Loops
seamlessly.

**Source text.** Jeremy Hartwood's diary (`doc07.txt`), as for 07.

## 20 - Ambient loop, cellar and caves (`20.ogg`, 3:00, seamless loop)

**Style.** The same idea underground: dripping water, a deep drone, far
drums once a minute, no melody. Loops seamlessly.

**Source text.** The pirate trial (`doc17.txt`), as for 11.

## 21 - Ambient loop, the dance hall (`21.ogg`, 2:00, seamless loop)

**Style.** The empty dance hall: a ghost of a waltz, as if a gramophone
played in the next room, through the same horn as 15 to 17, so faint it
could be the wind. Loops seamlessly. The scripted records 15 to 17 replace
it when the player plays one.

**Source text.** The game's captions, as for 17.
